# The 3 As of Cybersecurity

- The 3 As form the core access-control model.
- Authentication.
- Authorization.
- Accounting.
- Each A answers a different question.
- Each A depends on the one before it.
- The model is also known as AAA.

## 1. Authentication: Who are you?

- Authentication proves a subject is who it claims to be.
- It runs before any access decision.
- It applies to users, services, and devices.

### Factors

- Something you know.
    - Password.
    - PIN.
    - Passphrase.
- Something you have.
    - Hardware token.
    - Smart card.
    - Authenticator app.
- Something you are.
    - Fingerprint.
    - Face.
    - Iris.

### Methods

- Password-based login.
- Certificate-based authentication.
- Biometrics.
- Federated login via OIDC or SAML.

### Failure Modes

- Weak or reused passwords.
- Credential stuffing.
- Phishing.
- Session theft.

### Defenses

- Require MFA on every sensitive path.
- Require factors from different categories.
- Treat two passwords as single-factor.
- Rate-limit failed attempts.
- Lock accounts after repeated failures.
- Alert on impossible travel and new devices.

## 2. Authorization: What may you do?

- Authorization decides what an authenticated subject may do.
- It maps an identity to a set of permissions.
- It runs after authentication succeeds.

### Models

- RBAC: permissions attach to roles.
- ABAC: policies evaluate attributes.
- ReBAC: permissions derive from relationships.
- MAC: the system enforces fixed labels.

### Principles

- Least privilege: grant the minimum needed.
- Deny by default.
- Separate duties across roles.
- Check every request, not just login.

### Failure Modes

- Broken object-level checks.
- Privilege creep.
- Overly broad roles.
- Confused deputy attacks.

### Defenses

- Centralize policy in one engine.
- Test permissions with automated cases.
- Review access on a fixed schedule.
- Revoke permissions when roles change.

## 3. Accounting: What did you do?

- Accounting records what subjects did.
- It is also called auditing.
- It creates the evidence trail for everything else.

### What to Record

- Who acted.
- What action ran.
- Which resource was touched.
- When it happened.
- Where it came from.
- Whether it succeeded.

### Requirements

- Centralize logs off the host.
- Synchronize clocks.
- Make logs append-only.
- Retain logs for the compliance window.
- Protect logs from tampering.

### Failure Modes

- Logs stored only on the compromised host.
- Missing or skewed timestamps.
- No alerting on suspicious events.
- Retention shorter than the audit window.

## How the 3 As Work Together

- Authentication is the gate.
- Authorization is the rulebook.
- Accounting is the camera.
- Strong auth without authz lets any user do anything.
- Strong authz without accounting leaves no evidence.
- Auditing without auth and authz records actions nobody controls.
