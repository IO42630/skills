# The 3 As of Cybersecurity

The 3 As are a model for access control.
- Authentication asks who you are.
- Authorization asks what you may do.
- Accounting asks what you did.

## Authentication

- Proves an identity before access is granted.
- Factors fall into three categories.
    - Something you know, such as a password or PIN.
    - Something you have, such as a token or phone.
    - Something you are, such as a fingerprint or face.
- Multi-factor authentication combines two or more categories.
- Weak authentication lets attackers impersonate legitimate users.

## Authorization

- Decides what an authenticated identity can access.
- Runs after authentication succeeds.
- Common models include:
    - Role-based access control, which grants rights by job role.
    - Attribute-based access control, which grants rights by attributes and context.
    - Mandatory access control, which enforces system-wide labels.
- Follow the principle of least privilege.
    - Grant the minimum access needed for the task.
    - Review permissions regularly.

## Accounting

- Records who did what, when, and where.
- Also called auditing, the third A logs activity.
    - Authentication events: logins, logouts, and failures.
    - Authorization events: permission grants and denials.
    - Resource events: reads, writes, and deletions.
- Logs support detection, investigation, and compliance.
- Effective logging requires integrity.
    - Store logs where tampering is difficult.
    - Retain them for a defined period.
    - Review them, not just collect them.

## How the 3 As Work Together

- Authentication without authorization proves identity but controls nothing.
- Authorization without authentication grants access to unknown parties.
- Both without accounting leave no trace for investigation.
- Together they form the core of identity and access management, or IAM.
